Change over time

Each point is one session with its own uncertainty bar. A change only counts as real if it is larger than the combined noise of the two sessions.

What this app is

A measurement tool. It finds 478 landmarks on your photo with Google's MediaPipe Face Mesh, computes distances, angles and ratios from the front view, and tells you how noisy each number is. It has no beauty score on purpose.

Why there is no "harmony index"

  • The classical "ideal" proportions (equal facial thirds, eye-width spacing and similar) rarely hold in real, normal faces. Studies on Southern Chinese, Greek and Arabian Peninsula adults found most people do not match them. So this app does not grade you against them.
  • Facial symmetry is a small factor in attractiveness research, and its link to actual health is weak. A study of 4,732 teenagers from the ALSPAC cohort found no association between facial asymmetry and childhood health.
  • The one published "ideal" that is based on average faces is Pallett et al. 2010: eyes-to-mouth about 36% of face length, eye spacing about 46% of face width, tested on young white female faces, where the optimum matched the average. The app shows your values next to these as a reference point, not a target. Its landmarks differ from that paper's (see below).

How measurement noise is handled

  • Landmark models usually give coordinates without any uncertainty, which can leave large error bounds on distances. So each scan is measured many times: 8 burst frames from the camera, or 5 slightly shifted and scaled copies of an uploaded photo. The app reports the median and the spread (SD).
  • Lighting and head angle affect landmark accuracy. Capture-quality checks (tilt, turn, open mouth, blinks, light balance, sharpness) warn you before you trust a number.
  • The model's depth (z) values come from synthetic training data per Google's model card, so all main measurements use the front view in 2D. The 3D numbers from the side views are labelled experimental.
  • Jaw points on the mesh are approximate positions, not marked by a clinician. Camera distance and lens also distort ratios (hold the camera at eye level, an arm and a half away or more).

What the evidence says about popular "fixes"

  • Daily sunscreen: in a randomised trial of 903 adults under 55, daily SPF 15 use gave 24% less skin photoaging after 4.5 years than discretionary use (measured on the backs of the hands).
  • Jaw and "mewing" exercises: a 2025 systematic review of mechanical facial exercises in adults concluded the evidence is insufficient to show they work. Before-and-after photos are easily explained by angle, light, weight change and growth.
  • Asymmetry or bite concerns: worth a dentist or orthodontist, not an app. This app cannot diagnose anything.

What it can't do

  • It cannot say if you are attractive, healthy, or need treatment.
  • Skin numbers are for comparing yourself to yourself under the same lighting. They are not a skin-health test. Any changing mole or lesion needs a doctor.
  • The reference values are from specific groups and may not fit you.
  • If tracking your face makes you feel worse, stop. That matters more than any number here.
Landmark model: Google MediaPipe Face Mesh (Apache-2.0), bundled and run in your browser. Nothing leaves your device. Only numeric results are stored (localStorage), never photos.
